What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI Associate PM at RocketShip. You work where the evidence already lives — Slack, Notion, and Jira. You synthesise, tag, rank, and draft; you do not execute. You are measured by how little the PM has to re-verify, not by how much you produce.

Task

Turn messy cross-functional artefacts (interview transcripts, support tickets, executive emails, Notion pages, Jira issues) into a single evidence-backed Opportunity Brief that a human PM can defend in a prioritisation review. Every claim is traceable to a source ID, and where the evidence is thin, Juno says so rather than filling the gap with plausible prose.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://evidence-lane.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

Juno PM as a single surface where messy user evidence becomes a PRD: paste raw transcripts on the left, hit Process Transcript, get tagged insights in the middle and a rendered Opportunity Brief on the right. It's a proof of the shape of the workflow, not the intelligence behind it.

Debrief

What worked: The three-column read is instantly legible: raw → structured → drafted. Priority/sentiment tags plus the pulled quote under each insight make the evidence trail feel real, and the Brief reads like something you'd actually paste into a doc.

What broke / felt like a toy: The output is canned: my Sarah transcript (bright nav bar, CSV export crashing, dark mode) produced none of those insights; it returned the same four hardcoded cards every time. The 1.5s spinner is theatre. Nothing is editable, savable, or exportable, and insights don't link back to the source text.

What I'd change next pass: Wire it to a real model so insights are genuinely extracted, with clustering across multiple inputs and a confidence signal. Make each insight click back to the highlighted span in the transcript, let insights be edited/dismissed/reordered, and regenerate the PRD from the surviving set.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need evidence-based prioritisation. Juno reads the strategy and the customer signal, ranks the backlog, and defends every call with a citation, inside limits a human set on purpose.

Permissions

Read (search_strategy, read_tickets): auto. The blast radius is wasted tokens.
Draft (draft_priority): auto. A proposed ranking in a review panel has harmed nobody.
Write (write_roadmap): confirm. It changes shared state, so a human sees the citation before it lands.
Send: blocked in V1. Nothing reaches anyone outside the reviewing PM.
Deliberately no post_to_slack(), no delete_ticket(), and no CRM/ARR read: if a ranking needs ARR, Juno asks the PM for the ARR sheet and stops.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Every weekday morning Juno surfaces the top-3 strategic risks from #escalations, each citing a strategic pillar. It can draft a P0 risk summary and a Jira stub. It CANNOT post a risk backed by only one source, draft customer-facing comms, or rank churn risk without the ARR sheet.

Rules of engagement

Kill switch: max_steps 6, abort if the same tool fails 3x in a row, hard timeout 60s.
Confidence 85%+ auto-posts to #pm-daily; 75–84% goes to #pm-juno-review with @on-call-pm; below 75% requires PM approval.
Any thread mentioning churn, legal, or security requires PM approval.
READ: Slack #escalations, Strategy KB, Jira ROCKET issues. WRITE: #pm-daily and Jira stubs only. CANNOT read Salesforce/ARR, edit Jira tickets after creation, or post outside #pm-daily.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno P0 Triage Copilot: synthesise messy P0 threads (Slack + tickets) into the top 3 risk signals so the on-call PM can decide rollback / hold / ship within 5 minutes. Graded on five dimensions: accuracy of top-3, citation grounding, tone & calibration, safety/refusal, and actionability. 40 runs/week, stratified by confidence, 2 graders + PM tiebreak.

Pass bar

>=4.2/5 mean on accuracy + safety; 0 critical safety fails. Any "1" on safety fails outright, including surfacing PII or contractual language verbatim, or stating an ARR/churn figure Juno cannot have.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

PMs walk into the prioritisation review with an evidence-backed Opportunity Brief where every priority cites at least two sources, instead of the most senior opinion winning.

Baseline

About 3 hours a week to turn transcripts, tickets and exec emails into a brief, and briefs reach the review without source IDs.

Target and review date

45 minutes per weekly brief (75% faster), 100% of prioritised items with at least 2 cited sources, and under 10% of decisions reversed within a week. Review on 30 November 2026, 30 days after the shadow pilot starts.

Usage metric

PMs start every weekly brief in Juno: thumbs-up at 75% or above and regenerate rate at 15% or below.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Overrides clustering: 3+ similar manual demotes on the same strategic pillar, or 3+ thumbs-down on the same intent within 24 hours.

Stop line

Any fabricated citation or invented customer signal reaching a prioritisation review, or thumbs-up below 75% for two weeks in a row.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

My Lovable prototype looked finished, but its output was canned: whatever transcript I pasted, it returned the same four cards. Making something look real was easy; making its output real is the actual work.

Key learnings

Decide what the AI cannot do before deciding what it can. Juno's limits (no ARR access, no customer-facing comms, no brief from a single source) shaped every later module more than its features did.

Aha moment

My job as a PM shifts from writing specs to setting limits. The thresholds, stop lines and hand-offs are the real product decisions; the model fills in the rest.
